# FASTCLIP: the x-moneyforwhiskas pattern at our speed (2026-09-30)

RESEARCH plus a paper-only strategy. No bot was started (a 45 s boot check only); this strategy cannot
run with real money (`__main__.check_fastclip` refuses micro-live, live and shadow whatever the
environment says). Script `scripts/research_fastclip.py`, report `results/fastclip_backtest.md`, lock
`data/replay/fastclip_locked.json`. The trader: `docs/trader_x-moneyforwhiskas_2026-09-29.md`.

**Answer.** The trader's pattern — small taker clips on whichever side BTC is moving toward, both sides,
held to settlement — has real information in its trigger (random clips of the same size lose 4–10 % of
volume in every period), and **at the trader's speed it pays in every period: +6 to +15 % of volume**.
**At ours it is about break-even and unstable: −9 % on test, +1.9 % on forward.** The whole edge lives in
the first fraction of a second after the spot moves; our orders reach the book after most of the reprice.
That is why copying them does not work as-is. Not a candidate for money; a paper app is ready but not
started.

## The rule (the research lock)

| | the trader | fastclip |
|---|---|---|
| market | BTC 5-minute, every window | BTC 5-minute (`assets`) |
| trigger | first tick of a move on a fast source | composite spot's 500 ms return ≥ 1.5 σ (sqrt-time scaled) |
| what it buys | the side the move points to | the same, while ask ≤ value − taker fee; value = the side's mid 1 s ago + the change in the market's own fair-value curve since then |
| order | takes the ask | FAK at the ask (no chase), up to 16 shares / $7, ≥ 5 shares |
| clips | ~43 per market, both sides in 97 % | one order in flight, the same side again after 1 s, ≤ 40 orders and $40 per market, both sides |
| timing | 8–240 s after the open | 8–240 s after the open (60–292 s left) |
| price | 0.10–0.90 | 0.10–0.90, spread ≤ 3c |
| exit | none, settles | none, settles |
| resting maker bids | yes (no edge, earns rebates) | not copied: no rebate tier at our volume |

Chosen from 108 rules (source composite / leading exchange × 250 / 500 / 1000 ms × 1.5 / 2 / 3 σ × edge
0 / 1 / 2c × limit +0 / +1c) by train + validation net, before test and forward were read.

**The same rule at zero latency** (fills at the book the decision read, as for a trader ~1 s ahead of
our feed): +$903 / +$108 / +$259 / **+$2,894** (+14.5 / +5.9 / +6.6 / +10.3 % of volume), 72–89 % of
markets two-sided, **Up + Down 0.99–1.01 — the trader's 0.986.** At our speed we pay 1.03–1.13 for the
pair: we are buying after the reprice.

**Random clips** (same size, budget and timing, no trigger, no value gate): −5.2 / −10.2 / −4.2 / −8.8 %
of volume. The trigger is information; our latency is what spends it.

**Robustness.** 102 of 108 rules are positive on train, 50 on validation, 46 on test, 67 on forward, and
only 35 on both test and forward. On all seven assets the chosen rule loses on validation, test and
forward (−1.6 % forward; BTC +2.1 % is the only asset clearly positive on forward, as the trader's
BTC-only choice would suggest).

**Regimes on forward** (the only out-of-sample period with volume): positive in the first two minutes
(+1.2c / share at 240–292 s left, +2.7c at 180–240 s), negative later; by price, 0.40–0.60 and
0.80–0.90 positive. Test is negative in every time bucket, so none of this is a filter to trust.

## Verdict and next steps

No reliable edge at our speed; a large one at theirs. What would change it is latency, not
parameters: the decision-to-book path is ~250 ms of our own plus the venue, and the archive's arrival
book (validated 500 ms proxy) sits after most of the reprice. The paper app uses the same venue_lag fill
model, so it would reproduce this backtest rather than answer the speed question; its value is checking
the plumbing and signal rate on live data. The real test would be the recorded-book audit of a live
run against venue-timestamped books (`scripts/record_ticks.py`), once the latency work
(docs/latency_2026-09-29b.md) has moved the order path closer to the zero-latency line.
