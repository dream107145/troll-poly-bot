# Latency, round two: the spot path and the process itself (2026-09-29, evening)

**Answer.** After the morning's lanes, fast signing and event-driven decisions, our own code costs
14 ms between decision and POST (was ~100). What remains on our side is (1) the spot feed path,
155 ms from the exchange's trade to our decision, and (2) the process being busy enough to be cut
off the venue's book feed at every resubscribe, which then blocked entries for ~35 s each time.
Both are addressed below; the composite change runs on paper first.

## Measured (live bot, 16 orders after the 14:58 UTC restart; medians)

| stage | now | morning | our code? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| exchange trade -> spot received | 155 ms | 118 ms | the feeds: Coinbase 53 ms, Bybit 97, Binance 125 from this VPS |
| spot received -> decision | 0 ms | 52 ms | fixed (event-driven) |
| decision -> POST started | 14 ms (sign 6.5) | 37 ms | fixed (lanes, coincurve); 1 ms GIL switch below |
| POST -> venue reply | 243 ms | 237 ms | Polymarket |

The composite spot is the **median** of Binance, Bybit and Coinbase. A move that Coinbase shows
first (it is the nearest) cannot move a median of three until a second exchange confirms it, so the
composite reacts at the second-fastest feed: that is the 155 ms.

**Per-exchange feed delay from this VPS** (own trade timestamp -> our receipt, 35 min recording,
5 assets): Kraken 17 ms, Bitstamp 33, Coinbase 53, Bybit 97, Binance 125, OKX 130.

**Which feed shows a shock first, in receipt time** (48 Binance 3-sigma 1 s moves, trades):
Coinbase −23 ms before Binance (p25 −215), Bybit −1, OKX +1, Kraken +25, Bitstamp +141. Kraken and
Bitstamp are near but trade rarely; on quotes (below) Kraken is no better than Coinbase.

**What each composite rule would have shown, on 22 min of top-of-book quotes** (receipt-time lead
over Binance's own quote at 40 3-sigma moves; triggers per hour of the rule's own series, and the
share Binance confirms within 1.5 s):

| rule | lead p25 / p50 / p75 | triggers/h | confirmed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| median of 3 (today's live rule) | −144 / **0** / 0 ms | 98 | 100 % |
| lead composite, 3 sources, 30 ms window | −299 / **−79** / −15 ms | 156 | 93 % |
| lead composite + Kraken | −268 / −82 / −5 ms | 212 | 94 % |
| Coinbase alone | −187 / −25 / +43 ms | 53 | 95 % |

The first 18 minutes alone read −187 ms at the median; the gain is real but its size varies with
the hour, which is what the paper A/B is for.

## CPU profile of the live process (py-spy, 60 s, non-blocking)

42 % of a core; loop lag p99 24 ms. Where it went: TWAP integration 10 % (a walk over ~1000
samples per market per tick), the venue book feed 8 %, the spot feeds 14 %, the composite view
built on every spot quote for the shock pre-check 5 %, order-flow features 4 % (three passes over
30 s of trades), sorting latency samples for the status report 4 %, the state snapshot 7 %.

**The rollover drops.** Every resubscribe (new markets, ~every 5 min) opens a second connection;
its 36 snapshots land in one burst and the venue closes the OLD connection as a "slow consumer"
(1013) — 5 times in the first 29 minutes. That connection was still the one the guard watched, so
each drop tripped FEED_DOWN and then the guard's 30 s cooldown: about 35 s of no entries per event.

## What changed (code; tests in `test_twap_prefix.py`, `test_spot_lead.py`, `test_book_feed_handover.py`)

1. **TWAP prefix sums** (`pricing/twap.py`): the integral over any window is one subtraction plus
   two edge pieces; identical to the walk to rounding (300+ random ranges checked). ~10 % of CPU.
2. **Cheap composite price** for the shock pre-check (`CompositeSpot.price`), single-pass order
   flow, the latency report sorted every 5 s instead of every second, book deltas trimmed to the
   kept depth. Together ~10 % more.
3. **GIL switch interval 1 ms** (`__main__`): an order handed to a lane waited up to 5 ms for the GIL.
4. **Book feed**: 4096-frame queue on our side so a burst is buffered here, not at the venue; a
   drop of the old connection while a successor is already delivering is no longer an outage
   (no FEED_DOWN, no cooldown). A drop with no successor still is.
5. **Lead mode for the composite** (`CompositeSpot.lead_window_ms`, env `TPB_SPOT_LEAD_WINDOW_MS`,
   default 0 = off): each source's quote is dated by its measured feed delay (kept live from trade
   timestamps); the composite is the median of the sources whose information is within the window
   of the newest (30 ms: under the 45 ms Coinbase-to-Bybit gap). A lone source more than 25 bps from the plain median is a glitch and the median
   stands. Off, nothing changes.
6. **Kraken adapter** (`--exchanges ...,kraken`): available, off — no gain over Coinbase on quotes.

## What is running where

- **`tpb-spotlag-paper-lead`** (new): the FAK paper bot's settings plus `TPB_SPOT_LEAD_WINDOW_MS=30`.
  A/B against `tpb-spotlag-paper-fak` over the same hours: shock decision age, orders, fills, then the
  recorded-book audit. The lead series triggers the pre-check ~1.6x as often; the engine's own gates
  still decide, so the question is fills and profit, not trigger counts.
- **`tpb-spotlag-live`**: still the morning's code. A restart (when flat) picks up 1-4 above: less
  CPU, no rollover blackout, ~4 ms less before POST. Lead mode for real money only after the paper
  A/B, by adding `TPB_SPOT_LEAD_WINDOW_MS: '30'` to its env.

## Not changed, and why

- Polymarket's ~240 ms POST processing and the exchanges' distance are outside the code.
- Signing is 6.5 ms of the SDK's order building and EIP-712 hashing; a hand-rolled encoder could
  make it ~1 ms, at the cost of bypassing the SDK's checks. Not worth 5 ms next to the 240.
