# Conditional dynamic hedge / pair-lock: BTC 5-minute paper strategy (2026-10-01)

Paper only. Nothing here touches real money; the triangle strategy has no real-money switch (`__main__` refuses
micro-live / live / shadow for it). Runs as its own bot, `tpb-pairlock-paper` (`ecosystem.pairlock.config.js`),
separate from `tpb-repricedmom-paper`.

## Summary

* **Hedging after an adverse move cannot create a pair profit, and in six days of data it never added value.**
  Buying the opposite token after A falls always locks in a pair cost above 1 (section B). Measured directly at
  every adverse event (item 19), the opposite token bought at that moment is worth less than its cost 1, 5 and
  10 seconds later and at settlement, in every chronological period, after the moves of 3, 5 and 10 cents, in
  every time-left bucket and at every reversal-score level. A partial hedge is the same thing scaled down.
* **Pair locks after a favourable move also lose:** the cheap opposite token is fairly priced (or a little
  overpriced) when the lock is available, so a lock caps the winner for a fee.
* **The model-gated conditional hedge (C) does not beat holding out of sample;** the unconditional hedge (B)
  is worse than holding in every held-out period.
* **The only change that helped out of sample is the entry gate** (enter only when the model's expected net
  value per share clears a threshold, after fees, slippage and the adverse selection measured on fills).
* Held out (validation + test + forward), per traded market with 10 shares under the calibrated fill model: Baseline (the current rule) -0.37; A (entry gate, no hedge) +0.46; B (hedge every adverse move) -0.42; C (conditional hedge, chosen on validation) +0.34. None of the positive figures is statistically distinguishable from zero.

## A. How the strategy works

States per market (one cycle at a time; `strategy/pairlock.py`):

```
NO_POSITION -> INITIAL_ENTRY -> MONITOR -> ADVERSE_MOVE -> HEDGE_EVALUATION -> HEDGING
            -> PARTIAL_HEDGE / FULL_HEDGE / PAIR_LOCKED -> SETTLEMENT
```

1. **Trigger.** A 1-second composite-spot shock of at least 2.5 sigma with the 3-second move agreeing (the
   replay's trigger; the live bot can run its millisecond flow signal instead, `TPB_TRIANGLE_SIGNAL_SOURCE`).
2. **Entry gate.** The trigger's side is bought only if its expected NET value per share clears `entry_edge`:
   `EV = P(win) - ask - fee(ask) - expected slippage - adverse-selection cost`, where P(win) comes from a
   logistic model on the market price, the spot-vs-strike z-score, spot momentum (1 / 3 / 10 s), the token's own
   velocity and acceleration and the book's depth imbalance, fitted on the TRAIN period only; slippage and
   adverse selection are measured on TRAIN fills. FOK at ask + 3c, 10 shares.
3. **Monitor.** Every tick (~200 ms) the position is re-valued at executable prices: quantity, average cost, A's
   bid, the opposite token's ask, depth, spread, quote age.
4. **Adverse move.** A's bid at least `adverse_min` (3c) below A's average fill price.
5. **Reversal confirmation.** A reversal score (the mean of six standardised components, each signed toward
   B: token velocity 1 s and 3 s, token acceleration, spot momentum 1 s and 3 s, book imbalance) must stay above
   `rev_thr` for `confirm_ms`. Equal weights; nothing in the score is fitted to outcomes.
6. **Hedge evaluation.** Every candidate quantity `q` (10 % ... 100 % of the UNMATCHED quantity, never a function
   of the loss) is valued: expected P&L, variance, worst case, capital, pair cost after, execution probability.
   The best candidate by the objective (section B) is sent as a FOK if its gain per share clears
   `min_hedge_edge`. A candidate whose pair cost stays under `max_pair_cost` is a **lock**; one above it is a
   **loss hedge**, allowed only after a confirmed reversal and only up to `max_loss_pair_cost`.
7. **Pair lock.** When the matched quantity covers the position and the effective pair cost is under
   `max_pair_cost`, the cycle is `PAIR_LOCKED` (no further trading in it); fully matched above that, it is
   `FULL_HEDGE` (a locked loss). Either way the cycle is flat and a NEW, independent signal may start a new cycle
   (at most `max_cycles_per_market`), if the risk limits allow.
8. **Settlement.** Every share settles at 1 / 0. P&L is attributed by leg (prediction vs hedging) and by
   exposure (matched pairs vs directional remainder).

## B. The mathematical model

Notation: A = the side bought first, B = the opposite token; `qA, pA` A's quantity and average effective cost
(fees included); `qB, pB` the same for B; `p = P(A wins)`; fee per share `f(x) = 0.07 x (1 - x)`.

* **Entry EV per share:** `EV = p - ask - f(ask) - s_e - a_e` with `s_e` the mean fill-minus-ask and `a_e` the
  mean (predicted - realised) win probability on TRAIN fills.
* **Settlement P&L:** `P&L_A = qA - C`, `P&L_B = qB - C`, `C = qA pA + qB pB` (both legs pay independently).
* **Pair cost:** `pA + pB` per matched pair. Locked profit on the matched pairs `m = min(qA, qB)`:
  `m (1 - pA - pB)`, earned whichever side wins.
* **Why a hedge after a loss locks the loss in:** buying B matches against A's bids, so `ask_B ~ 1 - bid_A`.
  After A's bid falls to `bid_A < pA`, the pair costs `pA + 1 - bid_A = 1 + (pA - bid_A) > 1`. A pair cost under
  1 is only available after a FAVOURABLE move of A.
* **Hedge EV:** adding `q` shares of B at expected fill `x = ask_B + s_h` changes the expected P&L by
  `q (P(B) - x - f(x))` -- exactly B's value as a standalone bet. The loss carried on A is sunk and does not appear:
  this is the formal version of "never chase the loss". Multiplied by the FOK fill probability.
* **Risk-adjusted objective:** `J(q) = E[P&L] - lam Var[P&L] - kappa q x`, with
  `Var = p (1 - p) (qA - qB - q)^2`. J is quadratic in q, so a partial hedge can be optimal:
  `q* = u + (P(B) - x - f(x) - kappa x) / (2 lam p (1 - p))`, clamped to `[0, u]`, `u = qA - qB`. With
  `lam = 0` the rule is pure expected value and the optimum is a corner (0 or all).
* **Hedge rule:** hedge `q*` only if `(J(q*) - J(0)) / q* >= min_hedge_edge`.
* **Risk constraints (hard):** market capital, initial / total / hedge shares, hedge actions per cycle, the
  market's worst-case loss after the trade, daily loss, max pair cost (locks), max loss pair cost (loss hedges),
  max spread, quote and spot age, minimum time left. A larger loss can only push a candidate past
  `max_loss_pair_cost` and block it; it never enlarges a hedge (tested: `test_never_chase_the_loss`).

## Method

* **Data:** `data/charts`, 1,972 BTC 5-minute windows (2026-09-23 .. 10-01) of ~213 ms samples of both books
  (touch and depth within 2c) and the composite spot; 1,596 usable (300 s of history before the open for the
  volatility). Millisecond questions use the tick recordings (`data/ticks`, 2.25 h).
* **Splits, chronological by window:** TRAIN 50 % / VALIDATION 20 % / TEST 15 % / FORWARD 15 %. The model, its
  scales and the execution constants come from TRAIN only; every parameter was chosen on VALIDATION only; TEST and
  FORWARD were read once.
* **No future data:** every feature reads samples at or before the decision (unit test perturbs the future and
  checks the features are unchanged); volatility comes from the 300 s before the window opens.
* **Execution (the paper venue's, reproduced exactly; a unit test drives the real `PaperExchange` on the same
  book path):** decision -> arrival after 143 ms (measured: pre-send 31 ms + half the 224 ms round trip) -> FOK
  against the book at arrival with 80 % of the touch taken by faster traders -> the **calibrated** survival rule
  (the live bot's since 19:49 UTC, fitted on 193 real orders by the other session): 1 s after arrival the book
  must still fill the size within the LIMIT, charged at the worse of the two prices -> the result one ack later.
  The older **2 s rule** (every matched level must still be there 2 s later) is reported as a pessimistic
  sensitivity: against real orders it voids fills whenever the ask ticks up inside the limit.
* **Trigger proxy:** the live bot's millisecond flow signal cannot be reproduced from 213 ms samples; the replay
  uses the 1 s spot shock, and the new bot runs the same trigger so its paper record tests the replay.

**More hedging is worse:** the variance penalty lam = 0.1 (the most hedging) is the worst setting in both bands; B (hedge every adverse move) loses to holding in every held-out period on the gated entries. Hedging buys a lower variance (B's 5th-percentile market and drawdown are smaller) and pays for it with the mean.

## F. Failure analysis

* **Losing scenarios.** The test period (09-29 03:10 .. 09-30 09:50 UTC): the gated entries lost -0.74 per market; sideways and
  noisy windows (A -0.26 / -0.11 per market); entries with 120-180 s left (-0.77, 12 markets). Worst single market
  about -8.5 (a 10-share first leg at ~0.85 that lost).
* **Incomplete hedges.** C's hedge orders filled 41 % of the time (9 / 22 held-out); a killed hedge leaves the
  first leg as it was (counted, logged, never re-placed as a resting order). Their markets were roughly neutral.
* **Expensive hedges.** B's completed hedges cost 1.11-1.14 per pair on average (up to 1.52): each locks in 11-14c
  a share plus fees; 95 such markets lost 1.25 each.
* **Liquidity.** 80 % of the touch is assumed taken by faster traders; with near-touch depth that is rarely binding
  for 10 shares, but it is part of every fill.
* **Latency.** Entry fill rates of 22-37 % for the gated entries: the gate selects moments when the token is
  about to move, and the 1 s survival rule kills orders the market moved past. The forward period's 22 % is the
  lowest.
* **Adverse selection.** Measured on TRAIN fills: 2.75c a share of predicted-minus-realised win probability
  (7.4c under the 2 s rule); it is subtracted from every entry's expected value.
* **Maximum drawdown.** A -63.5 (train, 205 markets), -42.8 (test). B has the smallest drawdowns and the thinnest
  tail (5th-percentile market -1.7 to -5.1 vs A's -6.9 to -7.9): that is what hedging buys, at -0.42 per market of
  mean in the held-out periods.

## G. Recommendation

* **Do not use hedging after adverse moves or pair locks to make money**: in this market they have no positive
  expected value at our latency and fees, with or without a model, in any regime or time bucket.
* **The $1 / market benchmark is not achievable on this evidence.** The best held-out result is the entry gate
  without hedging: +0.46 per market (151 markets; validation +0.70, test -0.74, forward +1.46) or, at the more
  stable 0.00 threshold, +0.19 per market over 484 markets. Neither is distinguishable from zero (t ~ 1.0-1.4).
* **Paper only.** The new bot `tpb-pairlock-paper` runs the strategy C exactly as chosen on validation (it will
  hedge rarely, because almost no hedge clears its expected-value bar) so its paper record can confirm or refute the
  entry gate on fresh data. Real money is not supported by anything here.
