# BTC trend-reversal hedge: a mandatory first trade, a price-gated hedge (2026-09-30)

RESEARCH plus a paper-only strategy, `btc_trend_reversal_hedge`. No bot was started; the strategy cannot
run with real money (`__main__.check_btc_hedge` refuses micro-live, live and shadow whatever the
environment says). Script `scripts/research_btc_hedge.py`, report `results/btc_hedge_backtest.md`, lock
`data/replay/btc_hedge_locked.json`.

**Answer.** Requiring one trade in every BTC market has negative expectancy: **the mandatory first
trade alone loses $0.09–0.30 per market in every period** (train −$16, validation −$18, test −$34,
forward −$278 over 1,049 markets). Adding the hedge does not rescue it: unconditional hedging loses
about the same (−$245 forward) and the price-gated hedge slightly more (−$318). The one part of the
flow that makes money is the entry taken on a real trend (TREND_UP / TREND_DOWN: +$16 on train, about
flat elsewhere) — and that is exactly the trade the "no strong trend required" rule dilutes with 5× as
many CHEAPEST_SIDE_FALLBACK entries, which lose in every period. Not a candidate for money; the paper
app exists for a live check of the plumbing, not because the replay supports it.

## The flow as implemented

1. **MARKET_START** on the first evaluation of a BTC window. Other assets are refused (`btc_only`).
2. **Observe** `initial_observation_seconds` (10 s). Then the side: the 5 s BTC return ≥ `trend_z`
   (1 σ) with the 1 s return agreeing → TREND_UP / TREND_DOWN; otherwise the cheaper ask →
   CHEAPEST_SIDE_FALLBACK.
3. **INITIAL_ENTRY**: `initial_entry_size` (5) shares, FOK at ask + 1c, ask ≤ `max_initial_entry_price`
   (0.65). A kill is retried after the bot's 2 s gap, `initial_attempts` (8) times; from the
   `initial_fak_after` (3rd) kill the order goes out as a fill-and-kill — the existing fallback. Stale
   data, the price cap, thin depth, the hourly cap and every risk limit still refuse it: the
   requirement is to try, never to override a protection.
4. Optional adds while the trend continues (`add_while_trending`, default 0).
5. **REVERSAL**: the 5 s return points against the held side by ≥ `trend_z` for
   `reversal_confirmation_seconds` (3 s) of consecutive observations; one tick never triggers it.
6. **HEDGE_CHECK** on every tick after that while unmatched: pair_cost = average first-leg price +
   opposite ask + fee(ask) + slippage (0.5c) + latency allowance (0.5c); HEDGE when ≤ `max_pair_cost`
   (`hedge_mode` gated), always (unconditional) or never (none). A WAIT is re-checked later.
7. The hedge is FOK, sized to the unmatched first-leg quantity (never more, `max_hedge_size` 0 =
   that); once matched nothing more is bought. Everything settles; no take-profit, no stop.
8. **MARKET_END** at settlement: first-leg cost, second-leg cost, matched / unmatched, total cost,
   payout, fees, settlement and net P&L (also in the state snapshot, `btc_hedge_ended`).

## Replay (BTC, realistic execution, 3 latency draws, fees included)

Same archive and execution model as every other study (venue book read 500 ms after arrival, FOK
with 5 shares behind the limit, taker fee per fill). `max_pair_cost` swept on train + validation over
0.94–0.98: **flat** (train −$46 to −$51, validation −$8 to −$11); the lock picked 0.98, the app keeps
the brief's 0.97 — the difference is noise.

Fees and slippage on forward: A $85 + $26, B $144 + $50, C $125 + $40 — against gross results that
are already negative before them in every variant.

**By how the side was chosen** (net $, n markets, forward): TREND_UP −0.5 (65), TREND_DOWN +1.6 (53),
**CHEAPEST_SIDE_FALLBACK −279 (931)**. Train: TREND_UP +9.4 (17), TREND_DOWN +6.2 (15), fallback
−31.7 (157). The trend entries are about break-even to slightly positive; the fallback entries — 85 %
of all markets — carry the whole loss. **By initial side**: both lose (forward UP −133 / DOWN −145).
**Reversal vs no reversal** (A, forward): markets with a confirmed reversal −402 (954), without
+124 (95) — but a 1 σ move against the position over 5 s happens in 91–97 % of windows, so the
"reversal" is nearly always and the split mostly says that markets which never moved against the
entry won.

**Why the hedge cannot fix it.** In C the hedged pairs cost 0.83–0.86 in prices (about 0.90 with two
fees): each completed pair does earn ~10c a share, and C's 59–70 % market win rate shows it. But the
gate only lets a hedge in after the opposite token has become cheap, i.e. after the first leg has
already lost most of its value; the unhedged 30–43 % of reversal markets and the fallback entries
that never get a cheap hedge lose more than the pairs earn. B hedges everything at an average pair
cost of 0.99–1.05 in prices (1.05–1.11 with fees): a guaranteed loss per matched pair, offset only by
the occasional unmatched leg that wins.

## Verdict

- **Requiring one trade per market creates negative expectancy**: −$0.09 to −$0.30 per market in
  every period, driven by the 85 % of markets entered without a trend.
- The hedge, gated or not, does not recover it; the gate that would (only hedge when the pair is
  cheap) fires exactly when the first leg is already the loser.
- Nothing in test / forward was used to choose anything; the pair-cost sweep is flat anyway.
- The one supportable change from train + validation alone is to **drop the mandatory trade** and
  keep only TREND entries (`require_initial_trade=0` keeps the trend logic; the fallback then never
  fires) — which turns the strategy into a slower spot-lag with no exit, already studied.

The paper app (`pm2 start ecosystem.btchedge.config.js --only tpb-btchedge-paper`) is defined and
not started. Real money stays off.
